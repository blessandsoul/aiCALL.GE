<p><strong>TL;DR:</strong> Pick a chatbot when customers type to you on WhatsApp, Instagram, or your site. Pick a voice agent when they phone you or you need to phone them. Most Georgian businesses end up running both on one shared brain.</p>

<p>Every week a shop owner asks me the same question: do I need an AI that answers chat, or an AI that answers the phone? The honest answer is that these solve two different problems. A chatbot handles people who write. A voice agent handles people who call. The trick is matching the tool to the channel that loses you the most money right now.</p>

<p>aiCALL, the AI employee layer from <a href="/contact">aiNOW</a>, ships both as one product. The chatbot lives on five channels at once. The voice agent answers and places calls in Georgian. They draw on the same business brain, so the choice is rarely either-or. Still, you should start with the one that fixes your biggest leak.</p>

<h2 id="how-customers-reach-you">Start With How Customers Reach You</h2>

<p>Before comparing features, map your inbound. Spend ten minutes pulling the numbers and the decision gets obvious.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Mostly text.</strong> If your Instagram DMs, Messenger threads, and WhatsApp messages outnumber your calls, a chatbot is your first hire. Most Instagram DMs in Georgia land after 7pm, when nobody is at the desk, and a bot replies in under a second.</li>
  <li><strong>Mostly calls.</strong> A clinic, a salon, a car dealership, a restaurant: these run on the phone. People call to book, to ask if you are open, to check a price. If you miss those calls, you lose the booking. That is voice agent territory.</li>
  <li><strong>You do the calling.</strong> Cold lists, callback requests, reminders before an appointment. That is outbound, and only a voice agent scales it past what one operator can dial in a day.</li>
</ul>

<p>For a deeper split on text versus phone economics, our <a href="/blog/georgian-voice-ai-agent-guide">Georgian voice AI agent guide</a> walks the full picture, and the existing voice agents vs chatbots 2026 piece covers the market data.</p>

<h2 id="what-each-does-best">What Each Tool Does Best</h2>

<p>A text chatbot is asynchronous by design. A customer can ask at midnight, walk away, and read the answer in the morning. It handles high volume cheaply, shows product cards with price and rating inside the chat, and captures a phone number when buying intent is high. It never sleeps and it costs a fraction of a salaried manager near 1500 GEL a month.</p>

<p>A voice agent is synchronous and live. It picks up the phone, speaks Georgian, checks a calendar or stock list, books the slot, and logs the call to your CRM. It captures the after-hours call that would otherwise hit voicemail and die. For a clinic, see how this plays out in <a href="/blog/voice-agent-clinic-reception">AI voice reception for a clinic</a>; for sit-down venues, <a href="/blog/voice-agent-restaurant-reservations">restaurant reservations by voice</a>.</p>

<h2 id="a-worked-example">A Worked Example: One Tbilisi Salon</h2>

<p>Picture a mid-size salon in Tbilisi. It runs an Instagram page with steady DMs and a single phone line that rings all day. Two leaks bleed money. Daytime DMs sit unread while staff are with clients, and evening calls go unanswered after the salon closes at 8pm.</p>

<p>The fix is not one tool, it is two on one brain. The chatbot answers Instagram and WhatsApp, quotes prices from the catalog, and offers a callback when someone wants to book. The voice agent answers the phone line, checks the calendar so it never double-books a chair, and takes evening calls until midnight. A receptionist who left for the day no longer means a lost client. The salon owner reads it all from one dashboard the next morning. <h2 id="when-you-need-both">When You Clearly Need Both</h2>

<p>Some businesses run on a single channel and can stay there for a while. A pure online store with no phone line may live on chat alone. A traditional clinic that takes every booking by phone may live on voice alone. The middle, where most Georgian SMBs sit, runs both.</p>

<p>The advantage of one platform is shared memory and one bill. A customer who typed on WhatsApp and later phones in does not start over, because the same brain holds the business identity, the product catalog, and the knowledge base. Outbound campaigns feed the same CRM as inbound calls, so a cold list can warm into a booked lead without a second system. For the outbound side, read <a href="/blog/outbound-sdr-voice-calls-georgia">outbound AI voice calls</a>.</p>

<h2 id="decision-checklist">A Five-Minute Decision Checklist</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Count last month's inbound DMs versus inbound calls. The bigger number names your first hire.</li>
  <li>Ask whether you place outbound calls. If yes, you need voice regardless of inbound mix.</li>
  <li>Check your peak. If most contact lands after hours, you are leaking, and both tools answer 24/7.</li>
  <li>Decide if bookings depend on live availability. Calendars and stock favor the voice agent's check-before-confirm step.</li>
  <li>Confirm you want one CRM and one dashboard. If yes, one platform beats two stitched tools.</li>
</ul>
